Back safety rules

  • Keep your back flat and your core tight. Move slowly and never force a stretch.
  • Avoid sit-ups, toe touches, heavy lifting and hard twisting.
  • Stop if you feel sharp pain, pain going down a leg, or numbness. See a doctor or physiotherapist before you continue.
  • Sit with your back supported while studying and stand up every hour.

Healthy habits

  • Drink water when you wake up and eat enough through the day. At your weight, the goal is stamina and strength, not losing weight.
  • Sleep by 9:30 pm so a 4:00 am wake-up does not leave you tired.

Build every plate this way

  • Half the plate vegetables and salad, one quarter protein (dal, chickpeas, soybean, paneer, egg or fish), one quarter roti or rice.
  • Do not skip meals. Have protein at every meal to stay full while you study.

Eat more

  • Dals, chickpeas, sprouts, soybean, saag, mustard greens, cauliflower, cabbage, carrot, radish.
  • Orange, guava, papaya, apple and banana. Roasted chana, makhana and a small handful of peanuts or almonds.
  • Fish (rohu) once a week, grilled or in light curry. Plain curd in small amounts, no sugar.

Cut down step by step

  • Sugar in coffee: half the amount for 2 weeks, then a quarter, then none. Keep coffee to 2 cups, before 2 pm.
  • Packet snacks: keep only one small pack a week, then stop. Keep roasted chana, makhana or fruit at home instead.
  • Fried and oily food: once every two weeks, with less oil in home cooking. Mutton only occasionally, lean and boiled or lightly cooked.
  • Sweets, biscuits, cakes, white bread, sugary drinks: only a small portion on special days.

Water and habits

  • Drink 2.5 to 3 litres through the day and finish by about 8 pm. Dinner by 6:30 to 7:00 pm.
  • Wash your face twice a day with a gentle cleanser, do not pick pimples, change your pillowcase every week and sleep 7 to 8 hours.
  • Your acne gets worse around your periods. Do not diet in that week: eat enough, including saag, dal and chana.

Food can help your skin, but it may not clear acne by itself. Since you have many pimples, a doctor or skin specialist can check you and treat it, especially if it leaves scars, becomes painful or does not improve in about 3 months. This guide is general, not medical advice.
